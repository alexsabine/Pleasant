# PX-05/06/07/08/10: Holding, attention and test discipline

The Phoenix relational-weave document makes a careful distinction: holding does not make another system open; it can make exploration safer. The document is **conceptual, not a theorem**. For Pleasant Crescent this suggests:
- practical help is offered without requiring users to share more of their lives;
- newcomers may choose a quiet public map, then opt into suggestions;
- an invitation is not successful merely because it was delivered; people may decline;
- visible representation is negotiated with the people and communities affected.

### Turn the heuristic into a fair experiment
The Phoenix P12/P13 proposals compare CRR-informed prompts/glossaries with **equally informative neutral process language**, blinded tasks and pre-set metrics. Apply to Pleasant Crescent after the first human-tested directory works:

A. Neutral relevance and age/cost filters; B. Bayesian verification/recommendation; C. Bayesian policy + **extra** declared history/Fisher-distance controller. Same records, same tests, same budget and no leakage. Audit calibration, valid result rate, accessibility, output burden, revision detection, correct abstention and user sense of control.

Do **not** claim C beats B before tests.

### Limited positive finding relevant to restraint
Phoenix SEC-OBS1-H showed that an observation-aware abstention check can identify corrupting-monitor conditions on a **declared synthetic** continual-learning experiment, while also reporting no improvement over the cheapest transferred-constant comparator. For Pleasant Crescent, a justified analogue is a fail-closed publication/verification rule where evidence quality is insufficient—without claiming the benchmark transfers.
