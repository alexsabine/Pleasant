How an update reaches the map

  1. Something new arrives. A member adds a place or photo or sends a correction. A website changes, or a calendar event appears.
  2. It is tidied and held. GPS data is stripped, duplicates are flagged, and the item waits in Pending.
  3. It is pre-checked overnight. Lumen's specialist model looks at photos, compares changed pages and drafts a one-line summary with a suggested action.
  4. A steward decides, at any time through Lumen or at the monthly round: approve, reject, or ask the member for more.
  5. It goes live and is sealed. The map updates, the listing's freshness clock starts, and the decision is written to the log.
  6. It is watched. As time passes the listing's confidence fades, and the review queue brings it back for a re-check before Lumen stops recommending it.

Onboarding and stewards

Who stewards the map

Founding stewards

  • Kim, Sasha, Daniel and Bill
  • Add or amend the map at any time, through Lumen
  • Run the monthly round for general updates
  • Invite new stewards and agree system changes

How a new steward joins

  1. Joins as a member and starts contributing
  2. Builds a reliable record, then becomes an editor
  3. Is invited by at least three of the four founding stewards
  4. Signs the steward agreement, spends three months alongside a mentor, and is recorded in the log

Later, once there are more stewards, an invitation needs a majority of all stewards, with care for which communities are under-represented.

Stewards can add or amend the map at any time through Lumen. A steward simply tells Lumen what changed, for example "Story time at the main library has moved to Thursdays at 10." Lumen drafts the change with its source, the steward confirms with one tap, and it is live and sealed in the log straight away. The monthly round is only for general updates: members' suggestions, changed websites and new calendar events.

The full ladder

Responsibility grows with a track record, never with popularity, and only by invitation. The figures decide who may be invited; stewards invite, the member accepts, and the server records it.

"Reliable" uses a cautious estimate of how often a person's contributions are approved: 20 approvals and no rejections scores 0.87; with 2 rejections, 0.75. Old results fade over a year. Details are in the repository, in research/11_stewardship_pathway.md.

Events and coordinating groups

Every event has a time, a place and an end, and past events archive themselves automatically. Lumen can do more than list events: it can help a group be at the right place at the right time.

Coordinating a group event

The group does

  1. An organiser asks Lumen: "Set up a tide-pool walk for our playgroup on Saturday"
  2. Picks a public meeting place from the map
  3. Members tap "I'm coming"
  4. Everyone adds it to their phone calendar, with directions

Lumen does

  1. Checks the meeting place is public and verified, never a home
  2. Suggests a time using official tide times and alerts
  3. Sends reminders only to people who asked for them
  4. Tells everyone if the time or place changes, then archives the event afterwards
  • Public community events, such as library programmes, city events or entries from The Quadruplicate's calendar, appear only after a steward approves them.
  • Group meetups are visible only to the people invited, and never appear on the public map.
  • Meeting places are always public, verified places. Only a count of people coming is shown, never names or children's details.
  • Every time carries its time zone, and a changed time or cancelled event reaches everyone who said they were coming.
  • Reminders are opt-in by email or phone calendar. Lumen never messages anyone who has not asked.

Built today: events with times, places, repeats and automatic archiving, and the This week page. To build: group meetups, "I'm coming", calendar downloads, reminders and tide-time suggestions.
