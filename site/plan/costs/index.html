What it costs once live

For 100 members, Crescent Compass costs about $404 to $972 a year, depending on how much people use Lumen. That is inside Kim's $1,500 ceiling at every level of use, with a spending cap as a guarantee.

How an AI call works

  1. A member asks. "Somewhere free and indoors for a 4-year-old on Saturday?"
  2. Our server prepares the request. It adds the published listings as a short catalogue, the member's saved settings if they allowed it, the last few turns of the conversation, and the tools Lumen may use. Nothing private is included.
  3. Claude Haiku reads it and asks our server to run a search, for example "indoor, free, ages 3 to 5".
  4. Our server runs the search on published listings only, applies the 75 percent rule, and hands back the matching records.
  5. Haiku writes the answer, naming the listings each sentence relies on. It appears word by word.
  6. Our server checks every sentence against the records, removes anything unsupported, and seals the answer in the log. If Haiku's answer fails the checks, Opus answers instead, or the plain rules-based answer is used.

We pay only for the text sent and received, measured in tokens, roughly three quarters of a word each. A typical turn sends about 12,000 tokens, mostly the catalogue, and receives about 700. With Haiku that is about $0.0025 a turn; with Opus about $0.051.

Why this design

Haiku at the front desk. It understands everyday questions and writes warm, short answers for about a quarter of a cent. Using Opus for every turn would cost $2,000 to $4,000 a year at typical use.
Opus where judgement matters. Photos, changed websites, the steward's digest and the hardest questions go to the most capable model. Jobs that can wait run overnight at half price.
Our server decides; the AI writes. Recommending, permissions and publishing are rules and published maths on our server. That keeps the AI cheap, replaceable and unable to override a person.
The whole atlas fits in each request. The catalogue is small, so Lumen always knows the full, current atlas and nothing else. No custom AI model has to be trained or kept up to date.
Spending caps make the ceiling a guarantee. A per-member daily limit of about 40 turns, and a yearly cap on AI spending, stop a busy month from overshooting. Past a cap, Lumen answers from its rules, still accurately.
It works with no AI at all. Switched off, Lumen still answers from rules and templates and every trust check still runs. The year then costs $289.

Crescent Compass is a locally governed atlas for families, neighbours and newcomers. It helps people notice and care for their shared surroundings, and it never claims to be a complete picture of a place or of the people who live there.
