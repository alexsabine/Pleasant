Legal considerations

In short: Crescent Compass fits the law in California, the US and the UK. Kim is far below the size at which US privacy laws apply, and a few simple steps cover the rest.

Who is who

Kim, private individual, CaliforniaOwns the site and decides what members' data is for, so she is responsible for it (the "controller").
Alex, UK sole trader (Honami)Builds and runs the site, and handles members' data only on Kim's instructions (the "processor").
Kim, Daniel and BillFull access to every account and all the data, at all times.

Who holds the accounts: two options

Option 1: Alex holds them for Kim (preferred, but only because it is easier for Kim)

  • Alex signs up for GitHub, the server, the domain, Anthropic and email, and is paid back from the yearly allowance.
  • Kim, Daniel and Bill are added with full access.
  • The contract says Alex holds the accounts on Kim's behalf and moves any of them into her name whenever she asks.

Option 2: Kim holds them

  • Kim signs up for each service in her own name and pays directly.
  • She adds Alex, Daniel and Bill with full access.
  • Equally lawful. More effort for Kim: about an hour to set up, and her card on each service. Alex is happy with either option.

Either way, Kim stays responsible for the data, because she decides what it is for. Who holds the login does not change that. Alex has full access while building and running the site, and the build uses test accounts only. When signing up, accept each service's standard data processing terms; Anthropic's include the UK transfer clauses.

What Crescent Compass is

Crescent Compass is a community map and noticeboard, with a guide. It helps people find places and helps groups arrange to meet, but it does not run, staff or supervise any activity. Whoever runs an activity is responsible for it, and parents stay responsible for their children. The terms of use say this in one plain sentence, so everyone knows where responsibility sits.

  • Public events come from organisers' own websites and calendars, are checked by a steward, and link back to their source.
  • Group meetups are arranged by members, always at public places. Only a count of who is coming is shown, and reminders go by email to those who ask. No text messages.
  • Photos show places, not people: no identifiable children, location data removed, and a steward approves each one. (Registering a US copyright agent, $6 every three years, is optional.)
  • Children: accounts are for adults aged 18 and over, and nothing is collected about children.
  • Members' data: anyone can download or delete their data at any time from My atlas.

Becoming a steward

  1. Track record: at least 20 approved items over six months, then a year as an editor.
  2. Invited by at least three of the four founding stewards. No number does this on its own.
  3. Signs the steward agreement online, by typing their name. The founding four sign it too.
  4. Mentored for three months, then full steward access, checked by the server on every action.
Read the draft steward agreement

Crescent Compass steward agreement, version 2026-10. Between you and Kim, who owns Crescent Compass. Draft for Kim's approval.

  1. A volunteer role. Stewarding is unpaid and voluntary. You are not an employee, there are no required hours, and you can step down at any time.
  2. Accuracy first. Add or change only what you have seen yourself or have a source for, and say which. Never guess: if something is unknown, it stays "unknown".
  3. What is recorded. When you add, change or approve anything on the map through Lumen, we record what you said, word for word, along with Lumen's draft, what you confirmed, the source and the time, with your name. Your ordinary questions to Lumen are not recorded.
  4. Why it is recorded. To keep a true history of every change, to check later how each entry held up, and to let the map's mathematics decide how soon each listing needs re-checking. Comparing your words with Lumen's draft also tests Lumen's accuracy, so you are never blamed for Lumen's mistakes.
  5. Your track record. How often your entries hold up is kept privately. Only you and the founding stewards can see it. It is never public, never ranked against others, and never grants or removes a role on its own. An entry that was right when you made it and changed later never counts against you. You can see your record and challenge any outcome.
  6. Privacy rules. Never add children's names or details, anyone's home, private individuals' details, or sensitive nature sites. Add nothing about a Nation's lands, people or programmes without that Nation's consent.
  7. Confidentiality and security. Keep members' information and unpublished items private. Use two-step sign-in, and never share your account.
  8. Conflicts of interest. Say if you run or work for an organisation on the map. Another steward approves changes to its listings.
  9. Your contributions. You give Kim a free, permanent licence to publish, change and share what you add, including under an open licence, so the map can outlive any one of us. You keep any rights you already have.
  10. Stepping down. Your access ends when you step down. Your name stays on the changes you made, because the map's history must stay true, and your contact details are removed.

The contract between Alex and Kim

One agreement, signed electronically, is valid in both countries. It covers:

  1. Scope and acceptance: the eight-week plan, with Kim's written sign-off at each milestone.
  2. Money: $10,000 fixed, in US dollars, paid by milestone. Running costs come from the yearly allowance, and any new paid service needs Kim's approval.
  3. Ownership: once paid, the code and content belong to Kim. Alex's existing tools are licensed to her, and open-source parts keep their own licences. Avoid the phrase "work made for hire", which has a special meaning in California employment law.
  4. Accounts: option 1 or option 2 above.
  5. Data: Alex acts only on Kim's instructions, keeps data secure and confidential, and tells her of any breach within 48 hours.
  6. Limits and ending: reasonable skill and care, liability capped at the fee, and either side can end it on notice, with payment for work done. If there is a disagreement: talk first, then mediation. English law, with any claim brought where the other person lives.
  7. Ongoing work: a separate short support agreement, with an agreed rate.

Because Kim signs as a private individual, UK consumer law expects plain, fair terms, and gives her 14 days to cancel, paying only for any work she asked to start sooner.

Tax, for Alex

  • UK: ordinary Honami income on Self Assessment.
  • US: no tax, because the work is done in the UK. Kim needs no tax form.
  • VAT: generally outside UK VAT. Invoice in US dollars, marked "Services performed wholly in the UK. Outside the scope of UK VAT."

Before members can join

  1. Sign the contract.
  2. Alex runs the UK fee self-check.
  3. Kim approves the Privacy page and adds a contact email.
  4. Publish short terms of use: what Crescent Compass is, 18 and over, photo rules, and how to ask for something to be removed.
  5. Set up the accounts as option 1 or 2, accepting each service's data terms.
  6. Secure sign-in, with two-step sign-in for stewards and Alex, and a one-page breach plan.
  7. Keep a one-page record of what data is held and who can reach it.

Legal summaries reviewed

  • US privacy thresholds: Civil Code 1798.140, California Privacy Protection Agency
  • Privacy notices: Business and Professions Code 22575–22577 (the owner is responsible, not whoever hosts the site); FTC
  • Security and breach notice: Civil Code 1798.82, California Attorney General
  • Children: FTC COPPA FAQs
  • AI and recording: bot disclosure law; website chats and California's wiretap law: Manatt, Covington; Anthropic Usage Policy, Anthropic data terms
  • Events, texts and email: FCC, FTC CAN-SPAM guide; youth organisations: Business and Professions Code 18975 (applies to groups that supervise children, not to a map)
  • Photos and licences: US Copyright Office, Digital Media Law Project
  • UK data protection: processors, contract contents, transfers, fee self-check; the regulator became the Information Commission on 30 September 2026 (LexisNexis)
  • Contracts: Electronic Communications Act 2000, California Civil Code 1633.7, Labor Code 3351.5, consumer cancellation
  • Tax: US source rules, IRS, HMRC place of supply
  • Domain: CIRA
