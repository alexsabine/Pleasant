# Steward trust: consistent with the Bayes-optimal map

Generated by `backend/examples/steward_trust.py`. Module: `backend/pc_core/steward.py`.

## The model in one paragraph

Each steward has a reliability r: the probability that an entry is right *when it is made*. It is a Beta posterior over their checked entries, weighted so an outcome counts half after a year, with a pooled prior (the group's own record, worth 10 entries), so a new steward starts at the group's level. The map's existing freshness model gives S(t), the chance a correct entry is still current after t days. A listing is recommended while r x S(t) > 0.75, the same Bayes act as before under utilities +1 / -3 / 0. Trust therefore changes how soon a listing is re-checked, never who may contribute. When a re-check finds a listing different, only the share explained by "wrong when entered" counts against the steward; the rest updates the category's change rate.

## Two uses, two numbers, on purpose

- Map decisions use the posterior **mean**, because with linear utilities the Bayes act depends only on the expected probability.
- Invitations to a new role use the cautious **lower 5% bound**. That is a governance choice, and it never acts on its own: stewards invite, the member accepts.

## Baseline and metric (rule 10)

Native baseline: every steward treated equally, at the group's pooled rate. Metric: Brier score of the predicted chance an entry is right, against later re-check outcomes. Per-steward trust is switched on only if it beats the baseline on real held-out months. In simulation it matches the baseline when records are short and beats it as they grow; a flat prior without pooling is worse when records are short.

## What must be recorded for this to work

For each steward entry: the steward, what they told Lumen, Lumen's draft, what they confirmed, the source and whether they saw it themselves (observed) or were told (reported), and the time. For each later re-check: who checked, the outcome (still right, changed since, wrong when entered, can't tell) and any dated evidence. Comparing what the steward said with Lumen's draft also measures Lumen's own accuracy, separately, so a steward is never blamed for Lumen's misreading.

Synthetic simulation only; no real stewards or listings are scored here.
